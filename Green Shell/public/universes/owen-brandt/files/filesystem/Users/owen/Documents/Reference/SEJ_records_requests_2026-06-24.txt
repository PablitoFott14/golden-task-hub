SEJ members' webinar: Records Requests That Get Answered
Wed 2026-06-24, 12:00-13:30 CT, online
Panel: media-law attorney (open-records appeals), investigative reporter (300+ state and local requests), SEJ FOI Task Force member. SEJ staff moderating. Slides and sample letters to come by email.

GENERAL
- Write it so a clerk can't misread it. One subject per request. Name the record type, the office that keeps it and the date range.
- Call the records officer before filing. Ask what the system is called and what fields it holds, then request the fields by name.
- Ask for records in the format they're kept in. A spreadsheet beats a PDF of a spreadsheet.
- Keep a log: date sent, who acknowledged, promised date, every follow-up. Appeals go better with a paper trail.
- Narrowing isn't losing. Offer a smaller first batch to get something moving.

FEDERAL FOIA
- 20 working days to decide, plus up to 10 more for "unusual circumstances." Real life runs longer. Ask for an estimated completion date in writing.
- Fee category and fee waiver are separate asks. News media: no search or review fees, duplication only, first 100 pages free. Claim the category and ask for the waiver anyway.
- Agency has to provide records in the form or format requested if readily reproducible (E-FOIA, 1996). Cite it.
- Missed deadline generally means no search fees (2007 law, revised 2016). Exceptions for unusual circumstances. Worth one line in the request.
- Expedited processing needs "urgency to inform the public." Rarely granted for environment stories, panel said, but asking costs nothing.

MINNESOTA (Data Practices Act, Minn. Stat. ch. 13)
- Government data presumed public unless a statute classifies them otherwise.
- Can't be required to identify ourselves or give a reason for public data (13.05, subd. 12).
- No fixed deadline for public data. "Appropriate and prompt manner," "reasonable time." Data about yourself: 10 business days.
- Inspection is free. B&W copies, 100 pages or fewer: max 25 cents a page. Beyond that, actual cost of search, retrieval and copying, staff time included. No charge for separating public from not-public data.
- Electronic data: they have to give an electronic copy if they reasonably can, but they don't have to convert formats. Ask for what they already use.
- Denials must cite the specific statute. Get it in writing.
- Cities, counties, school districts and watershed districts are all covered. Each keeps a data access policy naming a responsible authority. Get that name before filing.

MY QUESTION (MN deadlines), answered near the end
- Treat 10 business days as the first follow-up and ask for a completion date in writing.
- After two missed dates, ask the Data Practices Office (Dept. of Administration) for an advisory opinion. Attorney said asking for one often moves things on its own.

WISCONSIN AND ILLINOIS, for comparison
- WI: "as soon as practicable and without delay." Location fees only if $50 or more.
- IL: 5 business days, one extension of up to 5 more. AG's Public Access Counselor reviews denials.

FOR THE COLUMN
- When a viral claim cites an agency, ask the agency for the data behind it, not the press release.
- Keep column requests short and specific. A quick partial answer beats a complete late one.
