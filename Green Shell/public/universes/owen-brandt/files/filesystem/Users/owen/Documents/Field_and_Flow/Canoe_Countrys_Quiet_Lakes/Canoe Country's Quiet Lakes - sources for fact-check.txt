Canoe Country's Quiet Lakes
Sources for the fact-checker
Owen Brandt, July 24, 2026

Figures and rules, in the order they appear in the piece:

1. About 1.1 million acres, more than 1,100 lakes, roughly 1,200 miles of canoe routes. Superior National Forest, BWCAW overview and trip-planning pages.
2. Around 150,000 visitors in a typical year. Superior National Forest visitor-use figures. The public affairs office in Duluth can confirm the current number.
3. "Often described as the most visited wilderness in the United States." A common description, used by the Forest Service among others. The piece presents it as a description, not a ranking.
4. Quota season May 1 through Sept. 30, with a set number of overnight permits per entry point per day. Superior National Forest permit rules and the Recreation.gov BWCAW page.
5. Group limit of nine people and four watercraft. BWCAW visitor regulations.
6. A rod is 16.5 feet. 40 rods = 660 feet. 200 rods = 3,300 feet. 240 rods = 3,960 feet, or 0.75 mile.
7. Forest Service advice on solitude: longer portages, off-peak travel. Superior National Forest trip-planning guide. Paraphrased in the piece, not quoted.
8. Early ice-out across much of northern Minnesota this spring. Minnesota DNR ice-out dates. My May story for the Great Lakes Dispatch, Ice-out on the Big Lake, has the Superior figures and their sources.
9. Motors banned on most lakes, still allowed on some lakes at the edge of the wilderness. Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness Act of 1978 and the current BWCAW motor-use map.
10. Airspace reservation, 1949, no flights below 4,000 feet above sea level. Executive Order 10092, signed by President Truman on Dec. 17, 1949.
11. Camping only at designated sites. BWCAW visitor regulations.
12. Fly-in resorts in the 1940s and the federal buyouts that followed. Thye-Blatnik Act of 1948 and the Superior National Forest history pages.
13. The 1978 act ended logging and phased out most motor use. Public Law 95-495.
14. If You Go box: day-use permits are free and self-issued at the entry point. Each designated campsite has a steel fire grate and a latrine. BWCAW visitor regulations.
